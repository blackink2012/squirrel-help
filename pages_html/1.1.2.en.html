Whenever your Maya assets need to be found again, reused, or shared by several people, Squirrel Asset Manager is useful.

Scenario 1: a personal material and model library

Collect the metals, fabrics and wood materials you use often, plus character, prop and furniture models, into your own library. Browse by sub-library and category, pick assets from their thumbnails, and import them into the current scene with a double-click or from the context menu.

Scenario 2: batch add downloaded assets to the library

Turn downloaded or created PBR texture sets, HDR/EXR files and model folders into .zasset packages in batches. Quick tools recognize texture types from naming conventions and generate thumbnails, so you skip creating assets one by one.

Scenario 3: reuse across projects

Use the project library to organize the assets of one project separately and archive them together when the project ends; put frequently used assets in favorites so you do not dig through folders every time.

Scenario 4: version and LOD management

When one asset needs several iterations or several levels of detail, keep history, proxy files and different resolutions as versions and LODs instead of copying and renaming files on disk by hand.

Scenario 5: cross-renderer material conversion

When materials must move between Arnold, V-Ray, Redshift and native Maya materials, material mapping and conversion reduce manual node wiring; for complex networks, still review the result after conversion.

Scenario 6: thumbnails, AI classification and search

Generate thumbnails for assets, let AI suggest categories, tags and Chinese names, then locate assets quickly by keyword, tag, natural language or image search.

Scenario 7: standalone browsing and remote driving

If you do not want to open Maya, browse and search assets in the standalone edition / EXE; when Maya is running, the standalone edition can also send import, export and thumbnail jobs to Maya through commandPort.

Scenario 8: images, music, animation data and sound search

Besides geometry and materials, scattered reference images, background music, sound effects and animation data (.atom) can go into the library too: images import as a file node or imagePlane, audio imports onto the timeline, the Time Editor or the Trax Editor, and animation data imports animation mapped by hierarchy onto selected attributes. To find "the music that sounds like that track", record system audio under the audio category; to find "the video that looks like this", record a section of the screen with the screen-recording search.
