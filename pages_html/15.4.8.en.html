One-line summary: the slider after the "Thumbnail:" label at the far right of the asset area toolbar controls card size — left for smaller cards and more per screen, right for larger cards and clearer detail.

Where it is

At the far right of the asset area toolbar: the text label "Thumbnail:" followed by a horizontal slider about 120 px wide. It is always there and needs no opening; in icon view dragging applies immediately.

What the two directions mean

Drag directionCardsColumns per screenGood for
Left (small)SmallerMore, up to 16 columnsScanning the library quickly for general shapes
Right (large)LargerFewer, down to 2 columnsInspecting thumbnail detail and comparing material texture

It defaults to about 8 columns. Internally the slider stores a "level": level = 10 - current columns, so more columns means a lower level.

What happens after dragging

ObservationDescription
Cards rearrange immediatelyThere is a short delay while dragging, then everything rearranges at once so the UI does not stall refreshing every card mid-drag
A wider window adds columns firstCard size is the "primary state"; a wider window prefers more columns rather than stretching cards
The recycle bin followsThe recycle bin page reuses this slider and the level applies to its cards too
Restored next timeThe level is saved with the interface state, so the next launch keeps the same size

It is the same thing as Ctrl+wheel

In icon view, holding Ctrl and scrolling the mouse wheel also zooms cards: up to enlarge, down to shrink, with the slider moving in sync. Both change the same "card size", so either works.

Submenus

The slider has no menu and no dropdown; it is a continuous adjustment bar.

Beginner pitfalls

  • Dragging the slider does nothing: most likely you are in list view. List rows have a fixed height, so thumbnail size mainly shows in icon view; switch back to Icons.
  • Thumbnails are too small to read: drag right, or Ctrl+scroll up. To return to default, drag to the middle-right (about 8 columns).
  • Assuming the slider changes "the thumbnail file itself": it only changes display size and never regenerates or modifies thumbnail images on disk.
  • Column count will not increase on a narrow window: the column count is also limited by window width; widen the window or collapse the side bars first.
