A playblast grabs the viewport quickly, a render uses the current renderer for high quality; both can use the current frame or the timeline.

Comparing the two sources

SourceSpeedQualityBest for
Maya playblastFairly fastViewport imageModels, scenes, assets that need an animated preview
RenderSlowerCurrent renderer qualityMaterials, lights, assets that need a clean cover image

Steps

  1. Right-click the asset and choose 「更新缩略图 → Maya拍屏」 (Update thumbnail → Maya playblast) or 「更新缩略图 → 渲染图」 (Update thumbnail → Render).
  2. Choose 「当前帧」 (Current frame) or 「时间轴」 (Timeline) in the frame mode dialog.
  3. Wait for the render. The current frame produces a single still thumbnail; the timeline produces a sequence compiled into an MP4 and also keeps the first frame as a still.
  4. It is written back as a thumbnail automatically and the card and preview refresh.

Execution details

  • A playblast temporarily turns off the camera gate and mask decorations and temporarily isolates the target object, restoring everything afterwards.
  • A render creates a temporary render layer that renders only the target object, then deletes the temporary layer and restores the scene state.
  • In the standalone edition or when driving a remote Maya, the job is delegated to the target Maya and the result is read back.

Verify the result

  • The card and the right-hand preview show the new image.
  • With the timeline chosen the thumbnail becomes an animation or multi-frame preview that can be stepped through in the preview panel.
  • After rendering, temporary isolated objects, render layers and camera decorations in the scene are restored.
警告

Rendering takes a while; do not operate Maya or switch scenes during a render or the output may be interrupted.

提示

Use a playblast to glance at the result quickly; use a render when you need a clean, presentable cover.
