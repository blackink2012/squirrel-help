While dragging an asset the thumbnail follows the cursor; snappable assets also show a translucent indicator disc and axes at the drop point, so you can see where it will land before releasing.

Prerequisites

  • The snapping toggle is on.
  • Every asset in the batch is a snappable type (models, dynamics, scenes, lights) and uses the same placement rule.

Steps

  1. Hold an asset card and drag it out of the plug-in window.
  2. Move the cursor over viewport surfaces and watch the trailing thumbnail and the drop indicator.
  3. Release once the drop point and orientation look right.

What you see

  1. Drag thumbnail: a roughly 80×80 card thumbnail follows the cursor, with its hotspot at the image center.
  2. Drop indicator: when the cursor hits a surface, a translucent square plane is drawn at the hit point with two axes inside and a white dot marking the exact drop point.
  3. Models, dynamics and scenes show local X/Z axes; lights show X/Y axes. The axis rotated onto the normal is not drawn.
  4. While a drop preview is visible the drag thumbnail fades to 25% so it does not cover the axes.

Verify the result

  • Moving the cursor over model surfaces moves the indicator, which follows the surface orientation.
  • The indicator hides when the cursor leaves hittable surfaces.
  • After releasing, the asset's orientation matches the axes shown in the preview.

Tips

  • The preview is a translucent overlay on top of the viewport: it creates and deletes no scene nodes, never enters the undo stack and never changes the selection.
  • Dragging materials, textures, HDRs, images, audio, animation data or node trees shows no drop preview, and neither does a mixed selection with different placement rules.
  • Images and audio do not go through viewport import at all (dropping creates no nodes), so a drop preview would be meaningless.
  • When the surface normal points almost straight down (−Y) the in-plane axes roll once abruptly; this is a singularity of the placement formula, not a drawing bug.
  • In large scenes the preview lowers its intersection frequency automatically, favoring drag responsiveness.
