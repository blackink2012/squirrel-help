One-line summary: these two are the only entry for "changing an asset's category", structured identically, differing in one sentence: move to = take it away, copy to = make another one.

What they look like

Opened, they are a cascading submenu: the first level is the 11 top-level categories (materials / models / lights / textures / scenes / HDR / animation / images / audio / animation data / node tree), and each level below expands that category's sub-categories.

Two ways to read it:

  • The item with the back arrow (an arrow pointing left) = selects "this category itself" without going deeper;
  • The All assets node does not appear in the cascade — it is not a real storage location.

Move vs copy

ScenarioWhich one
The asset really belongs in another categoryMove to
The same asset should be findable in two categoriesCopy to
Keep the original and try a modified versionCopy to (then edit the copy)
Save disk spaceMove to

With a multi-selection

Right-clicking Move to / Copy to with several assets selected applies to all selected assets — a whole batch moves at once. The tree refreshes and stops on the target category so you can keep organizing.

Notes specific to these items

  • Cross-sub-library moves are allowed: a material can move into models, a texture into HDR; the menu does not block it. But an asset of the wrong type sitting in another sub-library may not open / preview as expected there, so use this carefully.
  • Think about the target first: choosing wrongly costs one more move, not a re-import.
  • Extra copies are yours to delete: there is no "undo the duplicate" button, so remove unwanted copies with Delete → Move to recycle bin.
  • Tree counts may lag: if counts do not change after a move / copy, click refresh on the toolbar or switch to another category and back.
